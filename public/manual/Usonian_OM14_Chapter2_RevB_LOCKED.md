## 2.1 Purpose

A successful guitar begins with an organized workshop, sharp tools, and repeatable processes. Expensive equipment is not required, but accurate tools and a disciplined workflow are.

This chapter identifies the workshop, tools, and jigs used to build the Usonian OM-14. It is intended as a practical reference rather than an exhaustive woodworking guide. Every tool listed has a specific purpose during construction, and many of the specialty jigs are referenced in later chapters.

The Usonian workshop philosophy is simple:

> Use machines to remove material efficiently. Use hand tools to achieve precision.

---

## 2.2 Workshop Requirements

A dedicated guitar shop does not need to be large, but it should provide a stable environment that promotes accurate woodworking.

The workshop should include:

- Solid workbench  
- Good overhead and task lighting  
- Dust collection  
- Electrical service for stationary machines  
- Comfortable bench height  
- Adequate tool storage  
- Space for glue-ups and assembly

Maintain the shop at approximately:

| Parameter | Recommended |  
|-----------|-------------|  
| Temperature | 65–75°F (18–24°C) |  
| Relative Humidity | 40–50% |

Consistent humidity is one of the most important factors in producing a stable acoustic guitar.

## 2.6 Clamps

A surprising amount of guitar building involves holding parts accurately while glue cures.

Recommended clamp types include:

| Clamp | Primary Use |  
|--------|-------------|  
| Cam clamps | Braces and linings |  
| Spring clamps | General holding |  
| F-style clamps | Blocks and fixtures |  
| Bar clamps | Larger assemblies |  
| Deep-throat clamps | Bridge installation |  
| Go-bar deck | Bracing operations |

Protect finished surfaces with cork, leather, or wooden clamp pads whenever appropriate.

## 2.9 Sharpening

Sharp tools are safer, produce cleaner cuts, and require less effort.

Recommended sharpening equipment:

- Coarse, fine, and extra-fine diamond stones  
- Honing guide  
- Leather strop  
- Polishing compound

Touch up cutting edges frequently rather than waiting until they become dull.

---

## 2.10 Safety

Safe work habits are essential in every stage of guitar construction.

Basic shop practices include:

- Wear safety glasses whenever machines are operating.  
- Wear hearing protection around loud equipment.  
- Use a respirator when sanding wood or working with fine dust.  
- Keep cutting tools sharp.  
- Use push sticks and guards on stationary machines.  
- Disconnect power before changing blades or router bits.  
- Maintain a clean, organized workspace.

## 2.11 Chapter Summary

This chapter introduced the workshop environment, essential tools, and specialty jigs used throughout the construction of the Usonian OM-14.

The emphasis is on practical, repeatable craftsmanship rather than collecting specialized equipment. A well-organized shop, a modest selection of quality tools, and thoughtfully designed jigs will produce better results than an extensive collection of seldom-used machinery.

The chapters that follow reference these tools and jigs as they are used during each stage of the build.
