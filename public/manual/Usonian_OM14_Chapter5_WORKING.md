# Chapter 5 — Building the Rim Assembly

# Usonian Guitar Company

## OM-14 Steel String Acoustic Guitar

### Master Build Manual

**Chapter 5**

---

## 5.1 Introduction

Chapter 5 covers construction of the rim assembly for the Usonian OM-14. The rim assembly includes the bent sides, neck block, tail block, kerfed linings, side reinforcement strips, and the prepared rim edges that will later receive the soundboard and back.

The goal of this chapter is to produce a stable, square, accurately shaped body rim that preserves the OM outline and provides clean gluing surfaces for the top and back.

The rim is built in an outside mold to hold the body shape while the blocks, linings, and reinforcements are installed. Throughout this chapter, the following orientation terms are used:

- **Top edge**: the edge of the rim that will receive the soundboard.

- **Back edge**: the edge of the rim that will receive the back.

- **Neck end**: the upper end of the body where the neck block is installed.

- **Tail end**: the lower end of the body where the tail block is installed.

- **Inside face**: the interior surface of the side.

- **Outside face**: the exterior surface of the side.

The top edge of the rim is treated as the primary reference during early rim assembly. The back edge is left slightly oversized so it can later be sanded to the 15-foot back radius.

---

## 5.2 Side Preparation

Before bending, inspect the side set for cracks, runout, grain defects, and thickness consistency. The sides should be matched for appearance and oriented so the best visual face is placed outward.

Mark the center of each side and identify the bass side and treble side before beginning layout. Clearly mark the waist, upper bout, lower bout, neck end, and tail end using light pencil marks or low-tack tape.

Bring the sides to final bending thickness before bending. Typical finished side thickness for the Usonian OM-14 is approximately **2.1–2.2 mm (0.083–0.087 in.)**, depending on species and stiffness.

The sides should be smooth, even, and free of machining marks before bending. Scraper or sanding marks are much easier to remove before the sides are bent than after the rim is assembled.

---

## 5.3 Bending the Sides

Bend the sides using a bending form, heating blanket, bending iron, or other controlled lutherie side-bending method.

Lightly mist the sides before bending if appropriate for the species being used. The wood should be flexible enough to take the shape without scorching, cracking, or crushing the fibers.

Begin with the waist, then work toward the upper and lower bouts. Avoid forcing the wood. If resistance is high, add heat and allow the wood fibers to relax before continuing.

After bending, allow the sides to cool and dry in the form so the shape sets as much as possible before removal.

When removed from the form, the sides should closely match the OM outline and should fit the mold without excessive spring-back. Minor spring-back is normal, but the sides should not have to be forced aggressively into position.

---

## 5.4 Preparing the Mold

Inspect the outside mold before installing the sides. The mold should be clean, square, and free of debris that could dent the sides or prevent the rim from seating properly.

Confirm that the mold matches the OM-14 body outline and that the centerline marks at the neck and tail positions are clearly visible.

The mold should support the sides evenly around the entire perimeter. Any clamps, spreaders, or mold hardware should operate smoothly and should not interfere with the installation of the neck block, tail block, or linings.

---

## 5.5 Installing the Sides in the Mold

Place the bent sides into the outside mold with the outside face of each side against the mold. Align the waist first, then seat the upper and lower bouts.

Bring the neck and tail ends together at the centerline. The side ends should meet cleanly without gaps or overlap. Trim the ends only as needed to achieve a clean fit.

Install spreaders or mold clamps as needed to hold the sides in place. The rim should sit naturally in the mold without twist. Check the top edge against a flat reference surface and confirm that the sides are fully seated.

The mold now becomes the working fixture for the rest of the rim assembly.

---

## 5.6 Preparing the Neck and Tail Blocks

Prepare the neck block and tail block from mahogany. The blocks should be square, clean, and large enough to provide structural support at the neck and tail ends of the body.

The neck block provides the structural foundation for the bolt-on neck joint and must be accurately centered on the body centerline. The tail block reinforces the lower end of the guitar and supports the end graft and endpin area.

Before installation, mark the centerline on each block. Confirm the grain orientation and make sure the gluing surfaces are flat and clean.

The blocks should be fitted so they contact the inside surfaces of the sides fully, without gaps.

---

## 5.7 Fitting and Gluing the Neck and Tail Blocks

Place the rim assembly top-down on a flat bench or reference surface. In this orientation, the top edge of the sides becomes the working reference.

Fit the neck block and tail block so they are flush with the top edge of the sides. The blocks will extend beyond the back edge at this stage. This extra material is intentional and will be removed later when the back edge is shaped to the 15-foot radius.

Before gluing, use a 15-foot radius template to trace the back curvature onto the neck block and tail block. Bandsaw the blocks slightly proud of this line. Do not cut directly to the final line. Leaving extra material allows the blocks to be brought to their final shape later in the radius dish.

Apply Titebond Original to the mating surfaces and clamp each block firmly in place. Confirm that each block remains centered on the body centerline and flush with the top edge of the rim.

Allow the glue to cure completely before removing clamps.

After curing, inspect the joints for full contact, clean glue lines, and correct alignment. The top edge should remain flat and continuous, while the back side of each block should remain slightly proud for later sanding.

---

## 5.8 Installing the Kerfed Linings

### Purpose

Kerfed linings reinforce the joint between the sides and the soundboard and back while greatly increasing the available gluing surface. They also stiffen the rim assembly without adding excessive weight.

The Usonian OM-14 uses **standard kerfed linings** made from spruce, mahogany, or basswood.

### Prepare the Linings

Inspect each lining strip to ensure it bends freely without cracks or damaged kerfs. Cut the four lining pieces slightly over length, leaving approximately **25 mm (1 in.)** extra at each end for final fitting.

Immediately before gluing, lightly dampen the kerfed lining with a sponge or spray bottle. The added moisture makes the lining more flexible, allowing it to conform to the upper bout, waist, and lower bout with less stress and reducing the chance of broken kerfs.

### Dry Fit

Beginning at the neck block, position each lining and trim it for a snug fit against the neck and tail blocks.

Install the **top kerfed linings** flush with the top edge of the sides.

Install the **back kerfed linings** approximately **0.5 mm (0.020 in.) proud** of the side edge. Leaving the back linings slightly high provides material that will be removed when sanding the rim to the **15-foot back radius**. This preserves the full thickness of the guitar sides while allowing the linings and blocks to be brought to the correct radius.

Verify that each lining follows the contour of the body without forcing the sides out of shape before proceeding with glue.

### Gluing the Linings

Apply an even coat of **Titebond Original** to the mating surfaces of the lining and side.

Position the lining and secure it with spring clamps, clothespin clamps, or purpose-built lining clamps. Begin clamping at one end and work progressively around the body to ensure the lining remains tight against the side with no gaps.

Use enough clamps to maintain continuous contact along the entire length of the lining. Additional clamps are often required around the waist where the curvature is greatest.

Repeat the process for all four lining sections, checking that the top linings remain flush and the back linings remain approximately 0.5 mm proud.

Allow the glue to cure completely before removing the clamps.

### Final Cleanup

Remove the clamps and inspect the linings for complete glue contact. Remove any glue squeeze-out with a sharp chisel or cabinet scraper.

Trim the lining ends flush with the neck and tail blocks.

The top edge of the rim assembly should now be flat and continuous. The back edge will remain intentionally oversized until it is sanded in the **15-foot radiusing dish**, where the back linings, neck block, and tail block will be brought to their final profile.

### Inspection Checklist

Before proceeding, verify that:

- All four lining sections are securely bonded.

- The top linings are flush with the sides.

- The back linings are approximately 0.5 mm proud of the sides.

- The lining ends fit tightly against the neck and tail blocks.

- There are no visible gaps between the linings and the sides.

- Excess glue has been removed.

- The rim assembly remains square in the mold and free of twist.

---

## 5.9 Installing the End Wedge

### Purpose

The end wedge provides a decorative transition between the two sides at the tail of the guitar while reinforcing the tail joint. The end wedge is fabricated from the same decorative wood used for the headplate and body binding.

### Prepare the Rim Assembly

After the kerfed linings have cured, remove the rim assembly from the body mold and secure it to the workbench. Mark the centerline of the tail joint.

### Lay Out the End Wedge

Lay out the end wedge so it measures **19 mm (3/4 in.)** wide at the top edge and tapers uniformly to **13 mm (1/2 in.)** wide at the back edge. Center the wedge on the guitar centerline.

### Cut, Fit, and Glue

Cut the recess, fit the wedge until the joints are tight, and glue it in place using **Titebond Original**.

After the glue has cured, trim the wedge flush with the sides of the guitar. Leave the top and back surfaces slightly proud so they will be brought flush during the radiusing operation.

Reinstall the rim assembly in the body mold before proceeding to the radiusing operation.

### Inspection

- End wedge is centered on the guitar centerline.

- Joints are tight with no visible gaps.

- End wedge is flush with the sides.

- Top and back remain slightly proud.

- Excess glue has been removed.

## 5.10 Radiusing the Rim Assembly

### Purpose

Radiusing the rim assembly establishes the final geometry of the guitar body, ensuring the soundboard and back fit the rim without gaps. The Usonian OM-14 uses a **15-foot radius on the back** and a **30-foot radius on the top**.

### Prepare the Rim Assembly

Before sanding, verify that the glue has fully cured on the neck block, tail block, and kerfed linings.

Secure the rim assembly in the body mold. Keeping the rim clamped in the mold provides support for the sides during sanding and helps maintain the body's shape throughout the process.

Using a pencil, draw light hash marks across the entire back edge of the rim assembly, including the kerfed linings, neck block, and tail block.

### Radius the Back

Place the rim assembly, still secured in the mold, into the **15-foot radius dish**.

Using light, even pressure, rotate the rim assembly back and forth across the sanding surface. Occasionally rotate the entire assembly **180 degrees** to promote even sanding around the perimeter.

Continue sanding until all pencil marks have disappeared and the entire back edge—including the sides, kerfed linings, neck block, and tail block—shows a continuous sanding pattern. The back edge should follow a uniform **15-foot radius** with no remaining high spots.

### Radius the Top

Apply pencil hash marks around the top edge.

Repeat the process using the **30-foot radius dish**. Using the same light back-and-forth motion, periodically rotate the rim assembly 180 degrees to ensure even material removal.

Continue sanding until the pencil marks have been completely removed and the entire top edge follows a continuous **30-foot radius**.

### Inspection

Before proceeding, verify that:

- The back edge follows a continuous 15-foot radius.

- The top edge follows a continuous 30-foot radius.

- The neck and tail blocks blend smoothly into the sides.

- The kerfed linings have been sanded evenly with no remaining high spots.

- Both edges are free of flat spots or abrupt transitions.

- The rim assembly remains square and free of twist.

Using the **15-foot** and **30-foot radius templates**, check the rim assembly at several locations around its perimeter. The templates should make consistent contact with the rim, confirming that the radius has been maintained from the neck block to the tail block without localized high or low spots.

Any remaining high spots should be lightly sanded in the appropriate radius dish until the templates fit uniformly around the entire rim assembly.

The next chapters cover construction of the back and soundboard. After the back and soundboard have been completed, they will be joined to the rim assembly.
