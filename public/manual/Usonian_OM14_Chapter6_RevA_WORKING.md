## OM-14 Steel String Acoustic Guitar

**Chapter 6**

## 6.1 Introduction

The back is the first major plate assembly completed for the OM-14. It is simpler than the soundboard, but it still has to be joined, thicknessed, braced, radiused, and prepared accurately before the body can be closed.

In this chapter, the bookmatched back plates are joined into a single panel, brought to final thickness, cut slightly oversized to the body outline, reinforced with a center strip, and braced to the specified 15-foot radius. The completed back should be clean, stable, lightly responsive, and ready to glue to the rim assembly in Chapter 8\.

This chapter also establishes several processes that will be repeated during soundboard construction in Chapter 7: plate joining, thickness sanding, brace preparation, radius-dish work, go-bar clamping, and final brace shaping. The back is a good place to develop consistency with these steps before moving on to the more complex top.

By the end of this chapter, the back assembly will be:

- Joined along a tight center seam.  
- Thicknessed to its final dimension.  
- Cut slightly oversized to the OM-14 body outline.  
- Reinforced with the decorative back center strip.  
- Braced and shaped to the 15-foot back radius.  
- Ready for installation onto the rim assembly during body closure.

The goal is a back that is structurally reliable, visually clean, and consistent with the geometry established for the Usonian OM-14.

---

## 6.2 Materials & Specifications

The back of the Usonian OM-14 is constructed from a pair of bookmatched quarter-sawn hardwood plates joined along the center seam and reinforced with four radiused spruce back braces. The completed back provides structural rigidity, establishes the 15-foot body arch, and contributes to the tonal character of the finished instrument.

### Back Tonewoods

The following species are used for the Usonian OM-14:

- East Indian Rosewood  
- Black Walnut  
- Genuine Mahogany

The wood should be quarter-sawn, properly seasoned, and free of checks, knots, excessive grain runout, or other structural defects.

### Back Plate Specifications

| Item | Specification |  
|------|---------------|  
| Construction | Two-piece bookmatched |  
| Grain Orientation | Quarter-sawn |  
| Moisture Content | 6–8% |  
| Finished Thickness | 2.4 mm (3/32 in) |  
| Back Radius | 15 ft |  
| Adhesive | Titebond Original |

The back is sanded to its final thickness before bracing and is not adjusted further during the voicing process.

### Back Braces

The OM-14 uses four quarter-sawn spruce back braces.

Recommended species are:

- Sitka Spruce  
- Engelmann Spruce

Brace stock should be straight-grained with the annual growth rings oriented vertically when installed. The braces are fabricated slightly oversized, radiused to match the back, and carved after installation.

### Back Center Strip

A spruce center strip is installed over the center seam before the back braces are glued in place. In addition to reinforcing the joint, it provides a clean, finished appearance to the interior of the guitar.

The grain of the center strip runs perpendicular to the center seam, maximizing its ability to reinforce the joint.

A convenient source of material is spruce cutoff pieces remaining from soundboard construction, making efficient use of otherwise unused tonewood.

### Quality Objectives

Before proceeding to body assembly, the completed back should:

- Have a tight, gap-free center seam.  
- Be sanded to a uniform thickness of 2.4 mm (3/32 in).  
- Maintain a true 15-foot radius.  
- Include securely bonded spruce back braces.  
- Have a properly fitted spruce center strip.  
- Be clean and ready for installation onto the rim assembly.

## 6.3 Preparing the Back Plates

The back begins as two quarter-sawn plates resawn from the same board and opened like the pages of a book. Proper preparation before joining ensures an attractive bookmatch, a strong center joint, and a stable back panel.

### Inspect the Plates

Inspect both back halves for:

- Straight, consistent grain  
- Quarter-sawn orientation  
- Checks, splits, knots, or other defects  
- Excessive grain runout  
- Mineral streaks or sapwood that may affect appearance

Trim away defects that could weaken the finished back or detract from its appearance.

### Establish the Bookmatch

Arrange the plates to produce the most pleasing grain symmetry. Once satisfied with the orientation, mark the inside faces and add alignment marks across the center seam so the plates can be returned to the same position throughout construction.

### Acclimate the Wood

Allow the plates to acclimate to the workshop environment before machining. They should be at the same moisture content as the remaining guitar components.

### Flatten the Plates

Verify that each plate lies flat without excessive cup or twist. Minor irregularities will be removed during thickness sanding, but severely distorted stock should not be used.

### Joint the Edges

True the mating edges using a shooting board and a No. 5 or No. 7 hand plane. Take light, consistent shavings until each edge is perfectly straight, square, and free of tear-out.

Place the jointed edges together and hold the seam up to a bright light. No light should be visible anywhere along the joint.

Finally, place the plates together without clamps. A properly prepared joint should close completely under light hand pressure and remain tight along its entire length.

Once the joint fits perfectly, the back plates are ready for gluing.

## 6.4 Gluing the Center Joint

A properly executed center joint should become nearly invisible after sanding and remain structurally sound for the life of the instrument.

### Equipment

- Titebond Original  
- Flat work surface  
- Waxed paper  
- Clamps or jointing jig  
- Cauls  
- Damp cloth

### Dry Fit

Verify:

- Tight joint  
- Flush surfaces  
- Proper alignment  
- Stable clamping

### Apply Glue

Spread a thin, even coat of glue along one jointed edge.

### Clamp

Apply only enough pressure to fully close the joint while maintaining alignment.

### Clean Up

Remove squeeze-out after the glue begins to gel.

### Cure

Allow the joint to cure fully before machining.

### Inspection

Verify:

- Straight seam  
- No gaps  
- Flush surfaces  
- Rigid assembly

---

## 6.5 Thickness Sanding

Thickness sand the joined back to a nominal finished thickness of **2.4 mm (3/32 in)**.

### Procedure

- Inspect the center joint.  
- Use light passes through the drum sander.  
- Alternate faces between passes.  
- Measure thickness frequently.  
- Hand sand to remove drum marks.

### Quality Check

The completed panel should:

- Be uniformly thicknessed.  
- Remain flat.  
- Show no sanding defects.  
- Have a clean center seam.

---

## 6.6 Cutting the Back Outline

Transfer the body outline using the master template.

### Procedure

- Align the template to the center seam.  
- Trace the outline.  
- Bandsaw approximately 2–3 mm outside the line.  
- Smooth the sawn edge.  
- Mark the brace locations.

### Quality Check

Verify:

- Outline is centered.  
- Panel remains oversized.  
- Centerline is visible.  
- Brace layout is complete.

---

## 6.7 Installing the Back Center Strip

The back center strip reinforces the center seam while adding a decorative detail to the interior of the instrument.

### Procedure

- Prepare the inside surface.  
- Fit the center strip.  
- Apply Titebond Original.  
- Clamp evenly.  
- Trim the ends flush after curing.

### Inspection

Verify:

- Strip is centered.  
- Fully bonded.  
- Flat.  
- Free of gaps.

---

## 6.8 Fabricating the Back Braces

The Usonian OM-14 uses four quarter-sawn spruce back braces.

### Material

- Sitka Spruce  
- Engelmann Spruce  
- European Spruce

### Prepare the Brace Stock

Mill the brace blanks oversized and free of twist.

### Layout

Position each brace according to the layout marks and identify its installation location.

### Quality Check

Verify:

- Straight grain.  
- Vertical grain orientation.  
- Oversized dimensions.  
- Correct identification.

---

## 6.9 Radiusing the Back Braces

Each brace is sanded to match the 15-foot radius of the back.

### Procedure

- Secure the 15-foot radius dish.  
- Mark each brace with pencil witness marks.  
- Sand until all witness marks disappear.  
- Test fit on the back.  
- Mark the installation orientation.

### Quality Check

Verify:

- Smooth radius.  
- Full contact.  
- No twist.  
- Proper identification.

---

## 6.10 Gluing the Back Braces

Glue the braces with the back resting in the 15-foot radius dish using a go-bar deck.

### Procedure

- Dry fit all braces.  
- Apply Titebond Original.  
- Position the braces.  
- Clamp with evenly spaced go-bars.  
- Clean squeeze-out.  
- Allow to cure completely.

### Inspection

Verify:

- Tight glue joints.  
- Proper alignment.  
- Continuous 15-foot radius.  
- Clean assembly.

---

## 6.11 Carving & Voicing the Back Braces

After curing, carve the braces to their final profile.

### Procedure

- Trim braces to final length.  
- Carve a rounded parabolic profile.  
- Taper the brace ends.  
- Flex and tap-test the back.  
- Sand away tool marks.

### Quality Check

Verify:

- Symmetrical brace profiles.  
- Smooth tapers.  
- Secure glue joints.  
- True 15-foot radius.  
- Responsive but rigid back.

---

## 6.12 Final Back Preparation

Inspect and prepare the completed back assembly before joining it to the rim.

### Final Inspection

Verify:

- Tight center seam.  
- Uniform thickness.  
- Oversized body outline.  
- Secure center strip.  
- Properly carved braces.  
- True 15-foot radius.  
- Clean surfaces free of glue and defects.

The completed back assembly is now ready for installation onto the rim assembly in **Chapter 8 — Closing the Body**.
